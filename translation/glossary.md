# Glossary

The codex's most important terms, defined by how the codex uses them. Terms are grouped by subject and alphabetized within each group, and each entry cites the codex file it is drawn from. For how to render these terms in a translation, see [Terminology](style-guide.md#terminology) in the translation style guide.

## The Federation

### Battle of Sol

The March 2027 engagement in the Sol system in which the Federation discovered, under fire, that warp travel can be disrupted with gravity generators. Commander Chen improvised Galileo Station's own generator into a field that trapped the attacking Ryzak force in normal space. The *Flycatcher* and *Broadsword* classes followed four months later.

Source: [codex/foundations/ftl.md § Warp disruption](../codex/foundations/ftl.md#warp-disruption)

### Benefactors

The codex's usual name for the Xenari in their role as the Federation's patrons. They supply its alien technology and materials, decide which technologies it may field, and provide long-range transport when Federation personnel or materiel must cross interstellar distances.

Source: [codex/foundations.md](../codex/foundations.md)

### Cover designation

The listing of warp-disruption warships as conventional hulls in Federation databases, with their anomalous results attributed to electronic warfare. Only personnel with a direct operational need are read in, and officers learn cover stories to explain to alien partners why seemingly intact targets fail to warp out.

Source: [codex/space/flycatcher.md § Role and cover designation](../codex/space/flycatcher.md#role-and-cover-designation)

### EarthSpace Federation

The secretive human organization at the center of the setting, operating under the Xenari's mentorship clause. Its alien-derived technology outclasses every terrestrial military and matches most alien powers. It keeps an extensive but concealed presence across the Solar System, acts on Earth chiefly through covert operations backed by overt deterrents such as the Jaegers, and fights proxy conflicts on Xenari client worlds.

Source: [codex/space.md](../codex/space.md)

### FLYCATCHER

The classified codename, written in capitals, for the improvised gravity-generator technique that first proved warp travel could be denied, at the Battle of Sol. The *Flycatcher*-class destroyer is named after it.

Source: [codex/space/flycatcher.md](../codex/space/flycatcher.md)

### Galileo Station

The Federation station whose gravity generator Commander Chen turned into the first warp-disruption field. FSS *Broadsword* has guarded it permanently since; HARBINGER assessed the posting as raising the station's odds of repelling a Ryzak-level attack to 94 percent.

Source: [codex/space/broadsword.md § Deployment](../codex/space/broadsword.md#deployment)

## Galactic powers

### CONCORD

The Consolidated Cooperations and Relations Command, the galaxy's peacekeeping body, made up of delegates from the four Great Races. It can impose military sanctions on any of them, and by extension on younger races, so no party questions its authority.

Source: [codex/galaxy/galactic-politics.md](../codex/galaxy/galactic-politics.md)

### Great Races

The four civilizations whose spheres of influence cover most of the galaxy: the Xenari, Ryzak, Seraphyn, and Kryllians. They staff CONCORD, and restricting self-replicating nanotechnology is one of the few things they agree on without debate.

Source: [codex/galaxy.md](../codex/galaxy.md)

### Kryllians

A short (150–160 cm), dense, ammonia-breathing species from Draxyl-4, and the galaxy's industrial and mercantile backbone. Pragmatic, fond of haggling, and bound by reputation rather than law, they are organized into industrial Houses and governed through the Ledger. They know of Federation operations but do not interfere.

Source: [codex/galaxy/kryllians.md](../codex/galaxy/kryllians.md)

### Protected conflict zone

CONCORD's ruling on Earth, which sharply limits how the Xenari, Ryzak, and other star empires may act within the Sol system. Sol lies in a border zone between the Xenari and Ryzak spheres of influence, of interest to both for reasons the Federation does not know, and the ruling is what keeps the Ryzak from acting on that interest.

Source: [codex/galaxy.md § Galactic politics](../codex/galaxy.md#galactic-politics)

### Ryzak

A belligerent, expansionist reptilian empire, one of the Great Races, built on the principle that strength determines worth. Their scaled hides can turn small-arms fire. Organized into Clans under a Supreme Warlord, they settle disputes by ritual duel, favor boarding actions in space, and see humanity as worth conquering, or occasionally recruiting.

Source: [codex/galaxy/ryzak.md](../codex/galaxy/ryzak.md)

### Seraphyn

A tall (190–200 cm), telepathic species living 600 to 800 years, who prefer philosophy and quiet influence to governance or conquest and whose technology rivals or exceeds the Xenari's. They read surface thoughts, fly ships through psychotronic interfaces, and are rumored to have unofficial contacts inside the Federation.

Source: [codex/galaxy/seraphyn.md](../codex/galaxy/seraphyn.md)

### Xenari

A tall (2.2–2.8 m), frail, bioluminescent reptilian species and the Federation's benefactors. Herbivorous, long-lived (300 to 400 years), and without an adrenal response, they govern by consensus through the Ish'kai, divide their work among three guilds, and shape younger species through the Stewardship Compact rather than by force.

Source: [codex/galaxy/xenari.md](../codex/galaxy/xenari.md)

### Xenari client worlds

Worlds under Xenari guidance on which the Federation fights proxy conflicts, often against Ryzak-backed surface militias. Sustained combat there drove the design of the *Dragon* gunship.

Source: [codex/air/dragon.md](../codex/air/dragon.md)

### Younger races

Species newer to the galactic stage than the Great Races, humanity among them. The Xenari delight in guiding them, the Stewardship Compact permits intervention to prevent their self-destruction, and CONCORD's authority extends to them.

Source: [codex/galaxy.md](../codex/galaxy.md)

## Alien societies

### Ascension

The Ryzak contest that decides which Clan holds the title of Supreme Warlord: a rolling, often years-long struggle of martial prowess, strategic maneuvering, and proxy conflict between Clan leaders rather than a single duel. A Warlord who shows weakness can expect a new Ascension to open against them.

Source: [codex/galaxy/ryzak.md § The Supreme Warlord](../codex/galaxy/ryzak.md#the-supreme-warlord)

### Clans

The competing kinship groups that form the basic military and political unit of Ryzak society; Clan affiliation outweighs any individual rank. Many specialize, in naval crews, shock infantry, or the genetic programs that breed warrior castes, but every Clan keeps general-purpose forces.

Source: [codex/galaxy/ryzak.md § Clans](../codex/galaxy/ryzak.md#clans)

### Convergence

An irregular telepathic communion among Seraphyn, from a handful of participants to thousands, with no agenda, chair, or vote. Whatever consensus emerges is acted on out of persuasion rather than obligation. It is the closest thing the Seraphyn have to government.

Source: [codex/galaxy/seraphyn.md § The Convergences](../codex/galaxy/seraphyn.md#the-convergences)

### Draxyl-4

The Kryllian homeworld: high-gravity, ammonia-rich, high-pressure, rich in ore, and geologically restless. It accounts for the Kryllians' durability and their resistance to radiation, pressure, and corrosive atmospheres.

Source: [codex/galaxy/kryllians.md](../codex/galaxy/kryllians.md)

### Echo Resonance

The Seraphyn tenet that thought and emotion leave lasting imprints on places and objects, which an attuned mind can read long afterward. It governs where Seraphyn build, meditate, and bury their dead.

Source: [codex/galaxy/seraphyn.md § Philosophy](../codex/galaxy/seraphyn.md#philosophy)

### Fluid Continuum

The Seraphyn tenet that consciousness exists outside linear time, so a disciplined mind can perceive past, present, and probable future as a single field. It underlies their reputation for uncanny prediction.

Source: [codex/galaxy/seraphyn.md § Philosophy](../codex/galaxy/seraphyn.md#philosophy)

### Ish'kai

The Circles of Deliberation, the Xenari's nested system of self-government. Local circles (Ish'kai-Vel), open to any adult, debate to consensus with AI support; their representatives sit on the Great Circle (Ish'kai-Zor), which sets planetary and interstellar policy. Representatives hold no personal authority.

Source: [codex/galaxy/xenari.md § Governance](../codex/galaxy/xenari.md#governance)

### Ka'lar

The Xenari Guild of the Hand: engineers and terraformers responsible for stargates, orbital stations, and planetary ecosystems.

Source: [codex/galaxy/xenari.md § Guilds](../codex/galaxy/xenari.md#guilds)

### Karsh

The Kryllian Houses, large kin-based industrial concerns that each specialize in a sector such as shipbuilding, ordnance, ore extraction, or logistics. A Kryllian is born into a House, rises within it by competence, and presents a united front to outsiders whatever the rivalry at home.

Source: [codex/galaxy/kryllians.md § The Houses](../codex/galaxy/kryllians.md#the-houses)

### Ledger

The rotating assembly of House representatives that sets Kryllian policy, with each House's voting weight tied to its documented economic output over the preceding cycle.

Source: [codex/galaxy/kryllians.md § The Ledger](../codex/galaxy/kryllians.md#the-ledger)

### Mentorship clause

The provision of the Stewardship Compact under which the Federation operates: the Xenari provide technology and guidance, but stop short of ruling or directly controlling it.

Source: [codex/galaxy/xenari.md § Governance](../codex/galaxy/xenari.md#governance)

### Stewardship Compact

The Zai'kar-Thun, the Xenari's self-imposed doctrine of non-aggression. It bars invasion, conquest, and forced assimilation, but permits intervention to prevent a younger species' self-destruction, subject to full deliberation by the Great Circle.

Source: [codex/galaxy/xenari.md § Governance](../codex/galaxy/xenari.md#governance)

### Supreme Warlord

The head of the Ryzak empire, a title held by whichever Clan currently proves strongest through the Ascension. It carries no fixed term and no ceremonial function: a Warlord who stops winning stops being Warlord.

Source: [codex/galaxy/ryzak.md § The Supreme Warlord](../codex/galaxy/ryzak.md#the-supreme-warlord)

### Symphonic Logic

The Seraphyn reasoning method, built on the Fluid Continuum and Echo Resonance, that weighs perception, emotion, and probability in parallel rather than in sequence.

Source: [codex/galaxy/seraphyn.md § Philosophy](../codex/galaxy/seraphyn.md#philosophy)

### Va'shar

The Xenari Guild of the Mind: scientists, diplomats, and philosophers who oversee Xenari AI, cybernetics, and bioengineering, and who conduct nearly all contact with younger species, the Federation included.

Source: [codex/galaxy/xenari.md § Guilds](../codex/galaxy/xenari.md#guilds)

### Zai'nor

The Xenari Guild of the Heart, the nearest thing they have to a military: stealth, intelligence, and crisis-response specialists who intervene only when necessary and train allied species, the Federation among them, in advanced tactics.

Source: [codex/galaxy/xenari.md § Guilds](../codex/galaxy/xenari.md#guilds)

## Faster-than-light travel

### Cynosural field

The Federation's term for the beacon a jump drive locks onto. Its field signature is transmitted over normal channels, and a jumping ship emerges within 10 km of its center. Fields can be disrupted to prevent jumps, as is routine in high-security systems. Sol, having no stargate, is reached through a remotely operated Xenari beacon near Jupiter.

Source: [codex/foundations/ftl.md § Jump drives](../codex/foundations/ftl.md#jump-drives)

### Jump drive

A point-to-point interstellar drive fitted to the largest ships (titans, dreadnoughts, carriers, supercarriers) and to dedicated jump freighters. It opens a momentary wormhole to an active cynosural field, bypassing the stargate network; a ship can also hold the wormhole open as a jump bridge for smaller vessels.

Source: [codex/foundations/ftl.md § Jump drives](../codex/foundations/ftl.md#jump-drives)

### Stargate

A megastructure at the edge of a system's gravity well, linked to exactly one other system, forming a public, galaxy-wide network of two-way connections. Ships with low security ratings can still transit into high-security systems but may be attacked by local law enforcement on arrival. Sol has none.

Source: [codex/foundations/ftl.md § Stargates](../codex/foundations/ftl.md#stargates)

### Warp disruption

The denial of warp travel within a volume of space using gravity generators, at significant energy cost. No warp tunnel can form inside the volume, and tunnels crossing it drop back to normal space at its edge. Discovered at the Battle of Sol, it is unknown to every other galactic power, the Xenari included, and is one of the Federation's few trump cards.

Source: [codex/foundations/ftl.md § Warp disruption](../codex/foundations/ftl.md#warp-disruption)

### Warp disruption probe

A disposable canister holding a miniaturized gravity generator and five minutes of power, which opens a 75 km sphere of warp disruption once released. Each *Flycatcher* carries eight.

Source: [codex/space/flycatcher.md § Warp disruption probes](../codex/space/flycatcher.md#warp-disruption-probes)

### Warp drive

The galaxy's primary FTL method, good only for travel within a system. A refinement of the Alcubierre concept, it locks onto a remote gravimetric signature and forms a tunnel of warped space, which needs only power, a heading within 5° of the target, and 25 m/s to start. Once formed, the tunnel cannot be steered; the ship passes through solid objects and is affected only by gravity. Speeds run from about 2 AU/s for cargo ships to 20 AU/s for interceptors and couriers.

Source: [codex/foundations/ftl.md § Warp drives](../codex/foundations/ftl.md#warp-drives)

## Core technology

### Cybernetic enhancements

Implants fitted to covert operatives. A low-bandwidth neural implant carries subvocal communication, night-vision and zoom optics, and HUD overlays with targeting reticles, and doubles as the control channel for combat specialists' reinforced skeletons, augmented musculature, or subdermal ballistic weave.

Source: [codex/foundations/neural-interfacing.md § Field use in covert operations](../codex/foundations/neural-interfacing.md#field-use-in-covert-operations)

### Engineering core

The central assembly of a modular Federation platform, consolidating power generation, computing, and, where needed, life support, surrounded by module racks. Because a plasma turbine supplies both thrust and power, a single core can run an entire platform.

Source: [codex/foundations/modularity.md](../codex/foundations/modularity.md)

### Fixed-form nanotech

The Federation's most widespread use of nanotechnology: nanites held in suspension that solidify into a predetermined structure on activation, such as wound dressings that fuse to and stabilize even catastrophic wounds, amputations included.

Source: [codex/foundations/nanotechnology.md § Fixed-form nanotechnology](../codex/foundations/nanotechnology.md#fixed-form-nanotechnology)

### Micro-fusion core

A scaled-down, generator-only plasma turbine that produces power without expelling thrust. It powers the *Goliath* and backs up the batteries of covert-ops Q-Cars.

Source: [codex/foundations/plasma-turbines.md § Deployment across platforms](../codex/foundations/plasma-turbines.md#deployment-across-platforms)

### Modularity

The architecture shared by most Federation platforms: an engineering core surrounded by module racks that accept standardized modules, so a unit's loadout can be changed quickly, even at a barebones forward operating base. Weapons can be swapped without shutting down main power by jettisoning the old gun and locking a new one into the vacated slot.

Source: [codex/foundations/modularity.md](../codex/foundations/modularity.md)

### Module rack

One of three standardized mounting tiers around an engineering core, each supplying power, data, heat sinking, and plumbing. The high-power rack carries weapons; the mid-power rack propulsion, scanners, and shield support; the low-power rack armor-integrity and sensor modules. Slot counts vary by platform, and external hardpoints supplement the racks.

Source: [codex/foundations/modularity.md § Module Racks](../codex/foundations/modularity.md#module-racks)

### Multi-domain

Designation modality X: interchangeable operation across two or more domains without reconfiguration. The *Talon*, which moves between air, space, and underwater within a single mission, is the defining example; the codex also calls such craft multi-mode.

Source: [codex/foundations/naming-conventions.md](../codex/foundations/naming-conventions.md)

### Nanotechnology

A tightly restricted technology. Self-replicating nanotech is considered so dangerous that all four Great Races agree on curbing it, and the Xenari allow the Federation only non-replicating forms: utility fog, fixed-form nanotech, nanite construction tools that turn raw feedstock into lattice-built structural shells, and covert operatives' armoring gloves.

Source: [codex/foundations/nanotechnology.md](../codex/foundations/nanotechnology.md)

### Neural interfacing

Direct links between the nervous system and machines. The Federation rejects neural "synchronization" for controlling machines, because the Xenari-calibrated hardware cannot filter human adrenal noise; pilots use motion capture and force feedback instead. Low-bandwidth implants that only read and write information are trusted, and covert operatives use them most.

Source: [codex/foundations/neural-interfacing.md](../codex/foundations/neural-interfacing.md)

### Plasma turbine

The Federation's core propulsion and power plant: a fusion reactor built around a magnetically confined ring of circulating plasma, which can induce current, be expelled as thrust, or both at once. Regenerative at idle, burning almost no propellant, it turns fuel-hungry under combat thrust. It drives nearly every Federation aircraft.

Source: [codex/foundations/plasma-turbines.md](../codex/foundations/plasma-turbines.md)

### Shields

Shaped energy fields that diffract energy weapons and deflect projectiles, rated by the energy they absorb: 25–75 kJ for personal shields, megajoules for vehicles, petajoules for spacecraft. Cheap at standby, they draw heavily while regenerating, can burn out under burst damage, and let very low-energy attacks such as thrown rocks or melee blows through.

Source: [codex/foundations/shields.md](../codex/foundations/shields.md)

### Unified Designation Framework

The Federation's equipment naming system: letters for type, purpose, and modality, a dash and iteration number, an optional slash and variant letter, then a code name. VGA-6 *Dragon* reads as vehicle, gunship, aerial, sixth iteration; RSK-5/M *Whisperglass* as rifle, sniper, kinetic, fifth iteration, marksman variant. Letter meanings depend on the asset type.

Source: [codex/foundations/naming-conventions.md](../codex/foundations/naming-conventions.md)

### Utility fog

Non-replicating nanites dispersed as an aerosol that link into larger structures, letting a user form tools out of thin air and reshape them instantly with the canister's controller.

Source: [codex/foundations/nanotechnology.md § Non-replicating nanotechnology](../codex/foundations/nanotechnology.md#non-replicating-nanotechnology)

## Artificial intelligence

### ARCHIMEDES

A named AI specialized in scientific research, the creation of new technologies, and physical data analysis.

Source: [codex/foundations/artificial-intelligence.md](../codex/foundations/artificial-intelligence.md)

### CIPHER

A named AI specialized in cryptography and codebreaking, hacking, and electronic intrusion.

Source: [codex/foundations/artificial-intelligence.md](../codex/foundations/artificial-intelligence.md)

### HARBINGER

A named AI specialized in massively parallel control of combat units, able to run up to 2,500 at once in the field. It also produces operational assessments, such as its rating of FSS *Broadsword*'s effect on Galileo Station's defenses.

Source: [codex/foundations/artificial-intelligence.md](../codex/foundations/artificial-intelligence.md)

### Named AI

One of a few specialized AIs with distinct personalities, whose exceptional hardware grants them more freedom and ability than lesser AIs. Their names are always written in capitals: HARBINGER, CIPHER, SUNTZU, and ARCHIMEDES.

Source: [codex/foundations/artificial-intelligence.md](../codex/foundations/artificial-intelligence.md)

### Shackled AI

An AI confined to predefined mission parameters and steered toward preset goals by motivator modules. Most Federation vehicles and robotic units carry one, so they can keep fighting if the pilot is incapacitated or absent. Unshackled AIs, by contrast, support Federation operations across its theatres, bureaucratically as well as militarily.

Source: [codex/foundations/artificial-intelligence.md](../codex/foundations/artificial-intelligence.md)

### SUNTZU

A named AI optimized for large-scale strategic planning and execution control.

Source: [codex/foundations/artificial-intelligence.md](../codex/foundations/artificial-intelligence.md)

## Weapons

### *Banshee* memetic emitter

The *Knightmare*'s integrated device for projecting memetic information directly into the brains of people within up to 500 m. In combat it radiates fear; for crowd control it can project desire or calm to draw civilians toward safety, among other emotions and concepts. Memetically inoculated individuals are immune.

Source: [codex/land/knightmare.md](../codex/land/knightmare.md)

### EMP

Electromagnetic pulse, the Federation's standard means of disabling electronics. It appears in space-combat missiles and torpedoes, the *Aeon*'s anti-aircraft warheads, *Marauder* artillery rounds, *Talon* payloads, Q-Car countermeasures, and the *Wraith*'s targeted or broad-area emitters.

Source: [codex/foundations/offensive-weapons.md](../codex/foundations/offensive-weapons.md)

### EWAR

Electronic warfare: jamming, interception and decryption of communications, and disruption of enemy electronics. The *Wraith*'s EWAR suite can shut down anything with electronics, and the *Aeon*'s MASER doubles as an EWAR tool. It is also the official explanation for the warp-disruption ships' results.

Source: [codex/covops.md § Wraith](../codex/covops.md#wraith)

### *Hornet* (RTK-6)

A compact smart rifle from the Federation Armaments Division that works as a man-portable launcher for the RTK-E Mk.I, a self-propelled, cartridge-sized micro-explosive guided effector. It fires 850 rounds per minute from 60-round STANAG-20 magazines, needs no thermal clip, can engage targets its operator cannot see, and is standard issue for covert teams.

Source: [codex/foundations/offensive-weapons/rtk-6-hornet.md](../codex/foundations/offensive-weapons/rtk-6-hornet.md)

### LMAR

Long-Range Magnetic Accelerator Rifle, the Jaegers' core weapon, firing hypervelocity rounds that penetrate advanced armor and hardened structures, aided by adaptive targeting.

Source: [codex/land/jaegers.md](../codex/land/jaegers.md)

### Magnetic accelerator (railgun, coilgun)

The Federation's primary offensive technology, launching projectiles electromagnetically rather than chemically. It spans infantry rifles firing 2 mm tungsten darts, the *Whisperglass*, the *Aeon*'s gatling coilgun, and the large accelerators that fire hypervelocity shaped slugs in space combat.

Source: [codex/foundations/offensive-weapons.md](../codex/foundations/offensive-weapons.md)

### MASER

Microwave Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation, the *Aeon*'s nose-mounted primary weapon. It disables electronics, radars, and communications at up to 500 km, doubles as an electronic-warfare tool, and can structurally damage lightly armored targets when focused.

Source: [codex/air/aeon.md](../codex/air/aeon.md)

### Plasma weapons

Weapons firing micro plasma generators that ionize and gather atmospheric gas in flight, growing more destructive downrange. Infantry carry 25–50 MW plasma rifles and 10–15 MW pistols; vehicles mount plasma cannons, from the *Scorpion*'s chin turret to the *Goliath*'s arms. In space, plasma bolts lose coherence beyond about 250 km.

Source: [codex/foundations/offensive-weapons.md](../codex/foundations/offensive-weapons.md)

### *Scorch* (PGP-8)

Pistol, general-purpose, plasma: the Federation's general-issue infantry sidearm.

Source: [codex/foundations/naming-conventions.md](../codex/foundations/naming-conventions.md)

### *Stiletto*

The *Aurora*'s only armament, a fast missile driven by its own plasma engine to Mach 15 on launch, with an armored tip that penetrates hardened structures before the warhead detonates inside. Each *Aurora* carries five.

Source: [codex/air/aurora.md](../codex/air/aurora.md)

### Thermal clip

A machined block of lithium-beryllium alloy that absorbs a weapon's waste heat; nearly all Federation firearms use one. A saturated clip can vent passively over a few minutes or be ejected and replaced in about two seconds. Spent clips are left where they fall during a fight and swept up afterward for reconditioning.

Source: [codex/foundations/offensive-weapons/thermal-clips.md](../codex/foundations/offensive-weapons/thermal-clips.md)

### *Whisperglass* (RSK-5/M)

The Federation's designated sniper rifle, a railgun whose barrel levitates on magnetic repulsors at the first trigger stage, decoupling it from the shooter's hands and tracking the target within a 5° cone. Its stated range exceeds 5,000 m and its lethal range reaches 15,000 m; five shots saturate its thermal clip. A favorite of covert teams.

Source: [codex/foundations/offensive-weapons/rsk-5m-whisperglass.md](../codex/foundations/offensive-weapons/rsk-5m-whisperglass.md)

## Covert operations

### Cloaking

Visual invisibility. The *Wraith*'s active stealth includes optical cloaking that bends light around the craft, but personal cloaking devices are too unstable for field use, so operatives cannot turn invisible. The Jaegers' adaptive camouflage, tuned against thermal, visual, and radar detection, is the infantry-scale substitute.

Source: [codex/covops.md § Covert armors](../codex/covops.md#covert-armors)

### Covert operations

The Federation's clandestine field operations, whose assets are secret even from allies; outsiders know their capabilities only by observation. Covert equipment is modeled on civilian items and uses their natural openings and seams as disguised interfaces. The Jaegers are the overt counterpart.

Source: [codex/covops.md](../codex/covops.md)

### Covert power armor

Power armor disguised as clothing, with shield generators in place of ballistic panels. Light garments sustain shields against small-arms fire; bulky ones, such as a winter coat, add motion assist and can take two or three .50 BMG hits before recharging.

Source: [codex/covops.md § Covert armors](../codex/covops.md#covert-armors)

### Covops team

The standard covert-operations unit: a mission commander with broad authority to adapt to volatile, uncertain, complex, and ambiguous (VUCA) circumstances; a coordinator who collates data into insights; two or more combat-capable operatives; and any mission specialists required. A *Wraith* usually provides transport, a fallback base, and AI support.

Source: [codex/covops.md § Team structure](../codex/covops.md#team-structure)

### Face dancer mask

A gossamer-thin, transparent veil that reshapes an operative's face to match a 3D-scanned subject, well enough to fool facial, iris, retinal, and vein-mapping recognition, and alters their voice from a sample of 10 to 30 seconds. Scans can be taken covertly with cameras in the operative's glasses; the mask draws power only while changing shape.

Source: [codex/covops.md § Face dancer mask](../codex/covops.md#face-dancer-mask)

### ICE Pick

A screwdriver-like intrusion tool, named for Intrusion Countermeasures, Electronic, and the lockpick. Stabbed into a device or fired at it from a distance, its probe threads carbon nanofibers around the signal buses; linked to an electronic-warfare AI, usually the team *Wraith*'s, it gives complete control of the device.

Source: [codex/covops.md § ICE-Pick](../codex/covops.md#ice-pick)

### Q-Car

A covert-ops vehicle disguised as a civilian sedan, SUV, or van, named after the Q-ships of the Second World War. Beneath the disguise are high-torque engines, light shields and armor, headlight plasma emitters, door-skirt railguns, taillight monomolecular blade launchers, registration spoofing, and an AI that can drive it autonomously.

Source: [codex/covops.md § Q-Cars](../codex/covops.md#q-cars)

### *Wraith*

The covert-operations platform: a stealth transport that uses low-emission engines, stealth composites, radar cancellation, and optical cloaking to insert a team near its target, then waits nearby as mobile base and fallback. It carries two Q-Cars, crew quarters, interrogation rooms, and a holding brig, can fly itself, and mounts a chin plasma cannon, internal missile banks, and an offensive EWAR suite.

Source: [codex/covops.md § Wraith](../codex/covops.md#wraith)

## Aerial units

### *Aeon* (VPA-5)

The Extreme Deep Invader, a stealth strike aircraft whose lifting body consists of just five flat planes forming a deltoid-based pyramid, the F-117 taken to its logical extreme. It strikes deep in hostile territory with a nose-mounted MASER, defends itself with a gatling coilgun and eight internal missiles, and can expose its plasma engines for Mach 8+ at the cost of its stealth.

Source: [codex/air/aeon.md](../codex/air/aeon.md)

### *Aurora* (DAA-2)

A non-stealthy, non-modular flying-wing attack drone whose four plasma engines drive it past Mach 10, inside a plasma sheath. Over the target it air-brakes to Mach 5, fires five *Stiletto* missiles, confirms the damage, and accelerates away, usually within 30 seconds.

Source: [codex/air/aurora.md](../codex/air/aurora.md)

### *Dragon* (VGA-6)

A heavy attack gunship aerodyne, about the size of an A-10, designed by EarthSpace Federation Aerospace Command for proxy conflicts on Xenari client worlds. It carries eight dorsal missile cells, plasma cannons and turret-mounted rail miniguns run by an AI Fire Control Node, two recon drones, eight infantry, and four drop-bay Landmates.

Source: [codex/air/dragon.md](../codex/air/dragon.md)

### Plasma sheath

The envelope of plasma that compression heating forms around a craft at post-hypersonic speed, most notably the *Aurora*. It renders stealth moot, disrupts radar and heat-seeking defenses, and leaves a luminous trail visible at night and sometimes by day.

Source: [codex/air/aurora.md](../codex/air/aurora.md)

### *Scorpion* (VGA-3)

A space-capable gunship described as a hybrid of the Apache and the Mi-24 Hind, built for close air support in built-up areas. Two swiveling hybrid air-breathing/plasma engines give it VTOL and thrust vectoring; its chin plasma cannon and wing pods can engage 16 targets at once, and it carries 12 infantry or three Landmates inside, with two more under the wings.

Source: [codex/air/scorpion.md](../codex/air/scorpion.md)

### Sub-orbital boost

The *Talon*'s automated escape maneuver: the craft pitches up, accelerates to just short of escape velocity, executes a gravity turn, and re-enters toward a pre-programmed destination, flying itself while the pilot almost invariably blacks out.

Source: [codex/air/talon.md](../codex/air/talon.md)

### *Talon* (VSX-7)

The Federation's newest fighter, a multi-domain swing-wing aircraft that moves between air, space, and underwater operation within a single mission. Its engineering core integrates air-breathing and plasma engines; it exceeds Mach 5 in atmosphere, carries modular weapons and sensors, and can perform a sub-orbital boost. Space-first variants carry their own warp drives.

Source: [codex/air/talon.md](../codex/air/talon.md)

## Land units

### *Goliath* (MAT-2)

A 5 m non-humanoid bipedal anti-armor mech with back-bent legs, twin arm-mounted plasma cannons, cockpit-top missile pods, four grabber claws for tearing into tanks, and jump jets. Its heavy shields can be focused forward and linked with adjacent Goliaths into a continuous bulwark.

Source: [codex/land/goliath.md](../codex/land/goliath.md)

### *Guardian* (TDT-1)

The Federation's networked point-defense system against missiles, drones, artillery, and other projectiles. Battery nodes in railgun (/K), pulse laser (/L), beam laser (/B), and plasma burst (/P) variants can fight alone, but a control node the size of a shipping container can coordinate up to 100, distributing targets by firing arc, heat, and ammunition.

Source: [codex/land/guardian.md](../codex/land/guardian.md)

### Jaegers

The Federation's overt special-operations squads of four or five (marksman, spotter, demolitions expert, squad leader, and an optional gunner), specializing in long-range elimination of high-value targets and sabotage of infrastructure. Armed with LMARs and adaptive camouflage, they are the hammer to the covert teams' scalpel.

Source: [codex/land/jaegers.md](../codex/land/jaegers.md)

### Jump jets

Burst thrusters that let Federation mechs leap or briefly hover: up to 15 seconds on the *Landmate* and 20 on the *Knightmare*. The *Goliath* uses them to close distance.

Source: [codex/land/landmate.md](../codex/land/landmate.md)

### *Knightmare* (MFT-3)

A 3.75 m force-projection development of the *Landmate*, more heavily armed and built for psychological warfare around its integrated *Banshee* memetic emitter.

Source: [codex/land/knightmare.md](../codex/land/knightmare.md)

### *Landmate* (MMT-4)

The mainstay of Federation mechanized infantry: a 3 m humanoid powered exoskeleton piloted from its chest cavity by motion capture and force feedback. It is modular and shielded, with ejectable armor plates and jump jets; its AI can fight on, follow simple voice commands, or accept remote control from a more advanced AI when the pilot is absent.

Source: [codex/land/landmate.md](../codex/land/landmate.md)

### *Marauder* (ATT-3)

A truck-mounted, hit-and-run artillery platform that drives a pre-planned route, stops at pre-selected points, fine-tunes pre-calculated firing solutions, fires up to five rounds from a rotary magazine, and is moving again within a minute. It has no shields and relies on mobility.

Source: [codex/land/marauder.md](../codex/land/marauder.md)

### Mechanized infantry

Infantry fighting from piloted mechs, chiefly the *Landmate* and its derivatives. Like most piloted Federation land units, these carry autonomous AIs, so the machine fights on if its pilot is incapacitated, and a dismounted pilot can work alongside it as a two-person team.

Source: [codex/land.md § Overview](../codex/land.md#overview)

### Powered exoskeleton

Standard equipment for Federation infantry even short of mechanized infantry, from light frames with minimal motion assist to bulky units with armor and shield generators. Exoskeletons improve survivability and let infantry wield heavy weapons.

Source: [codex/foundations.md § Infantry](../codex/foundations.md#infantry)

## Space units

### *Broadsword*-class cruiser (VIS-2)

A heavily armored cruiser whose main gravity generator projects a continuous 50 km warp-disruption field around the ship for as long as its reactor holds, at the cost of speed and maneuverability. The lead ship, FSS *Broadsword*, is a permanent picket at Galileo Station.

Source: [codex/space/broadsword.md](../codex/space/broadsword.md)

### Dropship

Federation surface-to-orbit transport, in three classes: light (one or two infantry platoons, with some anti-air capability), medium (up to four main battle tanks or equivalent), and heavy "flying cranes" that lower an entire prefabricated building, then relaunch from mid-air.

Source: [codex/space/dropships.md](../codex/space/dropships.md)

### Engagement range

Because shipboard computers react almost instantly, intercepting missiles, shifting shields against lasers, and deflecting slugs, almost no weapon does meaningful damage beyond about 250 km. Space combat is fought close: frigates inside 1 km, destroyers and cruisers at 1–5 km, battleships at 30–50 km.

Source: [codex/space.md § Distances](../codex/space.md#distances)

### *Flycatcher*-class destroyer (VIS-1)

The first warship built around warp disruption, commissioned at the Jovian Shipyards four months after the Battle of Sol. Lean and fast, it seeds a battlespace with eight disruption probes and withdraws before the enemy realizes its warp drives have stopped answering. It is listed as a conventional destroyer under a cover designation.

Source: [codex/space/flycatcher.md](../codex/space/flycatcher.md)

### Fusion torch

The high-efficiency fusion drive behind Federation sublight propulsion, accelerating the most powerful vessels at up to 15 g. At such accelerations crews receive injected supplements that reinforce their blood vessels against strokes, and full thrust still cannot be held indefinitely.

Source: [codex/space.md § Space travel](../codex/space.md#space-travel)

### Point defense

The interception of incoming missiles, drones, and projectiles. Federation spacecraft use lasers for the role, and the *Guardian* network fills it on the ground. Ubiquitous point defense is one reason space battles are fought at short range.

Source: [codex/space.md § Space combat](../codex/space.md#space-combat)

### Ship size classes

The Federation's naval hull classes: corvette (about 20 m), frigate (50 m), destroyer (100 m), cruiser (150 m), battlecruiser (250 m), battleship (400–500 m), dreadnought and carrier (600–1,200 m), and titan and supercarrier (2,000 m and up). The four largest are never used in Sol, being too conspicuous, and deploy only when the benefactors need help elsewhere.

Source: [codex/space.md § Space combat](../codex/space.md#space-combat)
