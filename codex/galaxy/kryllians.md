The _Kryllians_ are a short, stocky, ammonia-breathing species from the high-gravity world of _Draxyl-4_, and the galaxy's industrial and mercantile backbone — their shipyards, factories, and merchant fleets keep the wider economy running.

## Physiology
Kryllians stand 150 to 160 cm tall, but their high-gravity homeworld has left them dense, powerfully built, and unusually durable for their size, able to shrug off physical strain and harsh environments that would cripple most other species. Their biology runs on ammonia rather than oxygen, so they need specialized breathing apparatus anywhere outside a Kryllian habitat — a limitation that has become an asset in toxic or corrosive environments no oxygen-breather could tolerate.

_Draxyl-4_ itself is a high-gravity, ammonia-rich world of extreme pressure, rugged terrain, and abundant metal ore, wracked by constant geological activity. Kryllians raised there are naturally resistant to radiation, pressure, and corrosive atmospheres, though travel beyond their homeworld still requires a controlled environment or a formulated breathing mixture.

## Society and Culture
Kryllian culture is pragmatic to a fault: function over form, reliability over elegance, efficiency over almost everything else. They take genuine pride in engineering and craftsmanship, and their dry, deadpan humor is often lost on outsiders entirely. They are not an expansionist people, but they are fiercely protective of their industrial hubs and trade routes, and once earned, Kryllian loyalty runs deep.

### The Houses
Kryllian economic and social life is organized around the _Karsh_, large kin-based industrial houses that each specialize in a given sector — shipbuilding, ordnance, raw ore extraction, logistics, and so on. A Kryllian is born into a House and, barring formal disownment or buyout by a rival, stays there for life; standing within a House is earned through demonstrated competence rather than birth order, so a skilled junior engineer can outrank a senior manager if their work speaks for itself. Houses compete fiercely with each other for contracts and territory, but present a united front to outsiders — undermining a rival House in front of a non-Kryllian is considered far more shameful than undermining them in private.

### The Ledger
Kryllians have no single ruler; influence over Kryllian policy is instead apportioned through the _Ledger_, a rotating assembly of House representatives whose voting weight is tied directly to their House's documented economic output over the preceding cycle. A House that underperforms loses influence at the next Ledger sitting, and one that overperforms gains it — a system Kryllians consider self-evidently fairer than heredity or popularity, and one that keeps the Houses locked in constant, productive competition with each other rather than open conflict.

### Negotiation
Haggling is close to a sacred art among Kryllians, and a deal closed too quickly is treated as an insult to both parties — proof that neither side properly tested the other. Formal negotiations follow an expected rhythm of opening offers, counteroffers, and ritualized complaints about the other party's unreasonableness, often stretched out over multiple sittings even when both sides privately agree on the final number well in advance. Rushing this process with an unfamiliar trading partner is one of the fastest ways for an outsider to earn a Kryllian's lasting suspicion.

### Reputation and trust
Kryllians deal constantly with species and governments that share no common legal system, so enforcement of a bargain usually comes down to reputation rather than law. A Kryllian House that reneges on a deal finds the fact circulated through the wider trade network astonishingly fast, and the resulting loss of future business is treated as a far harsher penalty than anything a court could impose. This makes Kryllians, paradoxically, some of the most reliable business partners in the galaxy — not from any particular virtue, but because a broken deal costs far more than it could ever save.

## Technology
Kryllian industry underwrites much of the galaxy's shipbuilding, weapons manufacturing, and infrastructure. Their signature strength is modular ship design — vessels built to be repaired, upgraded, or reconfigured quickly, which keeps them in demand across merchants, militaries, and independent operators alike. Kryllian mass-production runs the gamut from personal sidearms to planetary-scale construction, and their merchant fleet is among the largest in the galaxy; without it, large stretches of galactic trade would simply stop moving.

Kryllians keep a defensive navy to protect that trade, built around heavily armored ships with redundant systems and devastating broadside firepower rather than speed or subtlety.

## Relations with Other Races
- _Xenari_: respectful but wary — Kryllians value Xenari stability but find their philosophising inefficient. The two frequently partner on large infrastructure projects, Kryllians handling the engineering while the Xenari plan for the long term.
- _Ryzak_: openly at odds, as Ryzak conquest sits against Kryllian interest in stability, but Ryzak war fleets still depend heavily on Kryllian-built logistics ships, binding the two together whether either likes it or not.
- _Seraphyn_: mutual respect without much common ground — Kryllians find Seraphyn abstraction impractical, while the Seraphyn value Kryllian engineering when it comes to putting theory into practice.
- Humans (EarthSpace Federation): seen as an up-and-coming but still isolated and secretive power. Kryllians know about Federation operations but don't interfere, and are likely to be among the first alien traders humanity meets — discreetly, and deniably.
