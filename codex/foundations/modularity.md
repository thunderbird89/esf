## The big idea

Most Federation platforms are *modular*, with an engineering core consolidating power generation and computing systems with life support (if necessary), surrounded by one or more *module racks* that accept modules via standardized high-/mid-/low-power connectors to extend functionality. These modules can be changed quickly even in forward operating bases with little engineering equipment, allowing units to change loadout and capabilities in response to shifting mission profiles.

## Module Racks

Surrounding the engineering core are the three module racks, with standardized attachment surfaces and connectors. The racks supply power and data to the attached modules, provide heat sinking, and contain the plumbing necessary for use of raw materials and charges as needed.

The racks have variable numbers of slots, depending on the host unit, accounting for differences in power output, CPU capacity, and physical size/layout.

### High power rack

The high power rack almost exclusively accommodates weapons and offensive devices, which draw large currents to power railguns, lasers, or plasma generators.

### Mid power rack

The medium power rack usually contains propulsion modules, various scanners, and shield support devices. These usually don't need as much power as weapons, but can generate high heat that needs to be vented before modules are damaged.

### Low power rack

The low power rack is reserved for modules that support armor integrity or the ship's sensor suites. These draw very little current and are usually the most basic modules that exist to support the basic functions.

## Switching modules

The standardized nature of modules and their unified control protocols, makes it extremely streamlined to switch them out, even in the field if necessary (although this does require some support infrastructure, so it's best done in something as barebones as an FOB). This is particularly true for weapons, which can be replaced without even shutting down the main power, by jettisoning the old gun, and bringing a new gun into position so that it locks onto the now-vacant slot.
