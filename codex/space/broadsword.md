The _Broadsword_-class cruiser, designated VIS-2, is the Federation's stationary answer to the same problem the Flycatcher solves at range: a warship built to hold a warp disruption field in place indefinitely rather than seed it and withdraw. Broader and more heavily armored than the Flycatcher, all sharp angles and heavy plating, it is built for presence and control rather than speed.

## Continuous disruption field
Where the Flycatcher expends probes, the Broadsword's main gravity generator projects a fifty-kilometer disruption field that moves with the ship and can be sustained for as long as the reactor holds. The trade-off is substantial: the generator's power draw and its own gravitational noise both cut into the ship's maximum velocity and maneuvering, making the Broadsword a poor choice for anything but a fixed picket. What it loses in mobility, it makes up in permanence — a Broadsword on station denies an entire volume of space to warp-capable forces for as long as it remains there.

## Deployment
The lead ship, FSS _Broadsword_, was assigned as a permanent warp disruption platform at Galileo Station following the Battle of Sol, where Commander Chen's improvised use of the station's own gravity generator first demonstrated the capability in combat. HARBINGER assessed the Broadsword's arrival as increasing Galileo Station's defensive capability by forty-three percent, raising the probability of successfully repelling a Ryzak-level attack to ninety-four percent — largely by guaranteeing that a hostile force caught in its field can no longer choose to disengage.

## Cover designation
Like the Flycatcher, the Broadsword is listed in Federation databases as a conventional cruiser hull, with its actual performance attributed to electronic warfare rather than disclosed. Very few personnel outside its own crew and its host station's command staff are read into what the ship actually does.
