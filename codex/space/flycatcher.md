The _Flycatcher_-class destroyer, designated VIS-1, is the Federation's first warship purpose-built around warp disruption, one of two classes quietly commissioned at the Jovian Shipyards four months after the Battle of Sol. Lean and fast even by destroyer standards, it takes its name from _FLYCATCHER_, the classified codename for the improvised gravity-generator trick that first proved warp travel could be denied.

## Warp disruption probes
Rather than carrying its own always-on disruption field, the Flycatcher fights by seeding the battlespace: each ship carries eight stationary disruption probes, deployable canisters holding a miniaturized gravity generator and five minutes of power. Once released, a probe opens a seventy-five kilometer sphere in which no warp tunnel can form, and any tunnel that intersects the sphere collapses back to normal space at its edge. A Flycatcher that empties its full probe complement can blanket a battlespace far larger than its own hull could cover with a shipboard generator, at the cost of each probe burning out in minutes rather than sustaining the field indefinitely.

## Role and cover designation
The class is built for speed and a hit-and-fade profile: seed the probes, then clear the area before the enemy fleet realizes its warp drives have stopped answering. In Federation operational databases, Flycatcher-class hulls are listed as conventional destroyers, and any anomalous engagement performance is attributed to sophisticated electronic warfare rather than warp disruption. Only crews and commanders with a direct operational need for the capability are read into what the class actually does — see [warp disruption]({{ '/foundations/foundations/ftl#warp-disruption' | relative_url }}) for why the Federation guards the underlying technology so closely, even from its own benefactors.

## Strategic significance
No known doctrine among the Xenari, Ryzak, Seraphyn, or Kryllians accounts for an enemy that can deny warp travel outright; each of the great races has spent millennia developing countermeasures against jump-drive locks and cynosural interference, but none against this. The Flycatcher's existence is one of the Federation's few genuine advantages over adversaries who are otherwise its technological equals or betters, and its deployment remains deliberately restricted to avoid tipping that advantage.
